Privacy Policy

Video Player · Last updated November 7, 2026

Video Player is a personal Windows desktop app used by its developer, Jack Zicrosky, to manage his own YouTube and Twitch channels. This policy explains what the app does with data from Google (YouTube) and Twitch.

What the app accesses

When you sign in with Google, the app asks for permission to manage your YouTube account (the YouTube Data API scopes for uploading videos and managing your channel). It uses this access only to:

  • upload videos you choose, with the titles, descriptions, thumbnails, playlists and captions you enter;
  • create, edit, start and end your live streams, and set their thumbnails;
  • show your streams' status, viewer counts, stream health and live chat, and send chat messages you type;
  • read your channel's name and handle so you can tell your channels apart.

With Twitch, the app uses the same kind of access for your Twitch channel: stream title, category and tags, stream key, chat settings and similar stream-management actions you start.

Where your data is kept

Everything stays on your own computer. Sign-in tokens, settings and stream details are stored in the app's folder on your PC. The app has no server. Nothing is sent to the developer or anyone else. The only data that leaves your PC goes straight to Google (YouTube) or Twitch, when you ask the app to do something there.

Sharing and selling

Your data is never sold, shared, transferred to third parties, used for advertising, or used to train AI or machine-learning models.

Google API Services User Data Policy

Video Player's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. The app also uses YouTube API Services; see the YouTube Terms of Service and the Google Privacy Policy.

Removing access and deleting data

You can sign out of any account in the app (Accounts… › Remove), which deletes its sign-in from your PC. You can also revoke the app's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions (Google) or in Twitch's Settings › Connections. Uninstalling the app and deleting its folder removes all data it stored.
